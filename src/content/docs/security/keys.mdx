The vault keeps your own SSH keys, for the servers you reach with them. A key is encrypted on your device before it leaves it, and only you open the vault: with your vault password in any browser, and without it on the devices where you kept your keys. tTerm keeps the encrypted keys, with each key's public half and fingerprint: it cannot open the vault or read a key. The vault is in **Settings** → **Keys**.

## How a server opens with your key

A server opens with your key when you move it from [tTerm for Mac](../../local/mac/) with **My key**, or with **Save my key** on the strip after `ssh`: see [Move to cloud](../../local/move-to-cloud/). When you open a terminal on it, the device you open it from signs the login, with your keys open there: your browser, or tTerm for Mac for its own terminals. No other device is asked, your Mac included. The device signs an SSH login with that key and nothing else, and the key never leaves it. tTerm checks the signature against the key's public half before it uses it.

## Make your vault

<Tabs>
	<TabItem label="In a browser">

In **Settings** → **Keys**, press **Add a key from a file** and choose your private key, the file without `.pub`. If the key has a passphrase, type it and press **Open the key**: the passphrase opens the key in this browser and goes nowhere. Name the key and press **Next**.

In **Make up a vault password**, type the password twice and press **Make the vault**. Then, in a browser that can keep them with a passkey, tTerm asks whether to keep your keys here, as in [Open it in another browser](#open-it-in-another-browser).

	</TabItem>
	<TabItem label="In tTerm for Mac">

The vault is made the first time you save a key: with Move to cloud, or with **Save my key** on the strip after `ssh`. In **Make up a vault password**, type the password twice and press **Make the vault**: no Touch ID is asked for it. This Mac keeps its own lock of the vault from then on, opened with Touch ID or your Mac's password.

	</TabItem>
</Tabs>

### The vault password

You make it up when the vault is made: at least 12 characters, and a phrase of a few words is easiest. While it is shorter, the window says how many characters are left. Letters other than A to Z work too, but a computer abroad may have no keyboard layout for them.

<Screenshot name="vault-password" alt="Make up a vault password: id_ed25519 goes to your vault, encrypted in this browser, and opens with this password in any browser. Password and Again typed as dots, with Show, at least 12 characters, and Matches; tTerm does not know this password and cannot recover it; Cancel and Make the vault." />

The password itself goes nowhere: your browser or your Mac turns it into a key, and tTerm gives your vault's encrypted key only to a device that shows it knows the password. tTerm does not know the password and cannot recover it: keep it in your password manager.

A wrong password says so, and how many tries are left. After ten wrong ones within an hour, tries pause for an hour; after thirty within a day, until that day is over. The window says when to try again.

**Forgot the password?** Set a new one where your vault opens without it: on your Mac, in tTerm for Mac, **Settings** → **Keys** → **Change the password**, with Touch ID; or in a browser where your keys are kept, in **Settings** → **Keys** too. If there is no such device, the keys in your vault cannot be opened any more, by tTerm either. The keys themselves usually stay on your Mac, in `~/.ssh`.

<Screenshot name="vault-forgot" alt="Forgot your vault password?: tTerm cannot remind you of it, but a new one can be set where the vault opens without it, on your Mac in tTerm for Mac (Settings › Keys › Change the password, with Touch ID) or in a browser where your keys are kept; with no such device the keys cannot be opened any more; Got it." />

## Open it in another browser

In a browser where your keys are not kept, **Settings** → **Keys** says **Your vault is closed** and has a **Vault password** field. A server that opens with your key shows the same field on its card, before its terminal is made: see [A server that waits for your vault](#a-server-that-waits-for-your-vault).

<Screenshot name="vault-open" alt="Settings → Keys in a browser new to the vault: Your vault is closed, only your vault password opens the keys, right in this browser; the Vault password field with the password typed as dots, Show, Open the keys, and Forgot the password? under it. Below: the Vault password row, the two keys and where the vault opens without the password." />

- **Type the vault password** and press **Open the keys**. The keys open right in this browser.
- **Approve it in tTerm for Mac**, handy on someone else's computer: no password is typed there. Where tTerm for Mac opens your vault, under the field is **approve on MacBook-Pro**, with that Mac's name: press it, and **Open your keys on this browser** asks the Mac at once. On the Mac, a window says which browser asks, when it signed in and from which address. Both screens show the same six digits: press **Allow with Touch ID** only if they match. **It's not me** ends that browser's sign-in, and **Settings** → **Devices** shows what else to check. A request lasts five minutes. **Type the password instead** takes you back to the field.

In a browser that can keep them with a passkey, tTerm then asks whether to keep your keys here, so that next time the password is not asked there: **Keep with Touch ID** (or Windows Hello, or a passkey), or **Only this time** on a computer that is not yours: then the keys close when you close the tab, or by themselves when they are not used for a while.

<Screenshot name="vault-keep" alt="After the vault password: Your keys are open in this browser. Keep them here with Touch ID, so that next time Touch ID is enough, or, on someone else's computer, Only this time; Only this time and Keep with Touch ID." />

A vault made before tTerm Web 0.1.26 that has no password yet opens as before: **Open** in **Settings** → **Keys** brings up **Open your keys on this browser**, with **Ask it to open your vault here** for your Mac's approval, and **Recovery code** with **Open**.

In tTerm for Mac, a Mac with no lock of your vault yet – a second Mac, a vault made in a browser, or after you signed out and in again – says **This Mac has no lock on your vault yet** and opens it once with the vault password, with no Touch ID. After that, Touch ID or your Mac's password opens it there.

## Where your keys are open

Once you have a vault, your computers, the servers with your key and those through your Mac open only on a device where you opened your keys yourself: with the vault password, with Touch ID where the browser keeps them, or with the approval in tTerm for Mac. Your computers are the machines with tTerm's agent, on macOS or Linux, and each Mac with tTerm for Mac. Signing in to tTerm with the code from your email is not enough for them, so whoever got into your email gets nothing of them, even while your Mac is on with its vault open. A server with tTerm's own line, connected with one command or moved with **tTerm's pass**, is not one of them: signing in is enough for it. With no vault, or one with neither a password nor a key, nothing is closed this way.

On a device where your keys are not open, such a machine has a lock in place of its **+**, its terminals and its log are dimmed, each with a lock, and their tabs start with a lock. Nothing of it reaches that device: not what its terminals show, not its commands in search or in the input line's history, not its log. Click the lock, a terminal or the log, and a card in its tab says why it is closed and opens your keys right there, the way this device opens them: the **Vault password** field, **Open with Touch ID** where the browser keeps your keys, or **approve on MacBook-Pro**. Then:

- the lock: a new terminal on that machine opens in the card's place;
- a terminal already running: it shows from where it is now, and no new one is made;
- the log: it opens.

On the **Machines** page, such a machine says **closed on this device**, and on the project's page each of its terminals says so in place of its folder. On both, the button for a new terminal has the lock, and the terminals are dimmed. **Remove** and **Move to another project** open your keys first, right in their window: the red **Remove web-01** and the button that moves the machine come once they are open.

<Screenshot name="closed-machines" alt="Machines where the keys are not open: MacBook-Pro, web-01 and staging say closed on this device, their Open a terminal has a lock, and their terminals P/MacBook-Pro · shop and P/web-01 · deploy are dimmed, each with a lock; db-1 in Client X is as usual. Each machine has Remove." />

However you opened them, your keys then open every such machine on this device, and only the terminal you asked for is made. A lock asks for one terminal at a time: the card of another lock takes the place of the first one's, and a card left behind another tab closes, with no terminal, when the keys open. When your keys close here, as in [When it locks again](#when-it-locks-again), the locks come back at once, and the tabs of those terminals turn into their cards; the terminals themselves go on in the cloud.

When you change the vault password or take a key out of your vault, your other devices open the keys again: by themselves, using a key of the vault, or with the new password where the vault holds no key. The device where you made the change stays as it was.

In tTerm for Mac, the Mac's own terminals and the servers through it open without your keys, as before; your other computers and the servers with your key open there once your vault is open, with Touch ID or your Mac's password. Your Mac signs in to a server with your key only for itself: a browser opens such a server once it opens your keys there.

## When it locks again

- **In a browser** – after 8 hours without anything done, when you close or reload tTerm Web, when you sign out, and when you press **Lock** in **Settings** → **Keys**.
- **In tTerm for Mac** – it stays open while tTerm runs, until you press **Lock** or sign out. Signing out also makes the Mac forget its lock: after you sign in again, it opens the vault once with your vault password.

## Settings → Keys

At the top, the section says whether the vault is open here, how it opened and when it locks again. Under it, **Vault password** says when the password was set, with **Change the password** beside it. Each key has a line: its name, kind and short fingerprint; the servers it **Opens**, or **No server opens with it yet**; when it was saved from a Mac or added from a file; and when it was last used, or **not used yet**.

<Screenshot name="keys" alt="Settings → Keys: the vault Open in this browser, opened with Touch ID, with Lock; Vault password, which opens the keys in any browser, set today, with Change the password; two keys with their kind and short fingerprint; Add a key from a file; and where the vault opens without the password: Chrome on macOS." />

- **Change the password** sets a new one, which replaces the old one at once: the old one no longer opens your keys. Your Mac and the browsers where your keys are kept open them as before. In a browser where your keys are kept, it asks for Touch ID (or Windows Hello, or your passkey); in a browser where they are not, for the current password; in tTerm for Mac, for Touch ID.
- **Remove** takes the key out of your vault, after a question, and tTerm deletes its encrypted copy. The servers that open with it stay in tTerm but no longer open. The key itself stays where it came from: on your Mac, or in its file. Like every change of your vault, it works only where your keys are open; elsewhere tTerm says so.
- **Add a key from a file** is there in a browser. In tTerm for Mac, keys come from Move to cloud and the strip after `ssh`.

Under the keys, the section says where the vault opens without the password, each Mac and each browser that keeps your keys: **Without the password your vault opens on: MacBook-Pro · tTerm for Mac and Chrome on macOS. In any other browser, with the vault password.** Where none keeps them, **Your vault opens with its password only: no device keeps it yet.**

A vault made before tTerm Web 0.1.26 that has no password yet shows **Set a vault password** at the top: **Set the password** makes one, and the recovery code stops working after it. In a browser where your keys are not kept, setting it takes your recovery code.

### The keys it keeps

Ed25519, ECDSA P-256 and P-384, and RSA. Not DSA or ECDSA P-521 keys, and not keys on a security key (`sk-…`), which cannot leave it: their servers open [through your Mac](../../local/move-to-cloud/#three-ways-in).

A browser reads a key in OpenSSH's own format, also with a passphrase, or in PEM without one (PKCS#8, PKCS#1), one key a file. A PEM file with a passphrase is first turned into OpenSSH's format with `ssh-keygen -p -f` and the file. tTerm for Mac reads keys in OpenSSH's format.

Some browsers cannot sign with Ed25519 keys. **Settings** → **Keys** says so there, and servers with such a key open in a newer browser or in tTerm for Mac.

## A server that waits for your vault

On a device where your keys are not open, a server with your key no longer waits in a terminal: its lock, or the button for a new terminal, opens its card first, before the terminal is made. The card says **web-01 opens with your key**, and the keys open right on it:

- In a browser where your keys are not kept, the **Vault password** field is right there: type the password and press **Open the keys and the terminal**. Under it are **approve on MacBook-Pro**, with your Mac's name, and **Forgot the password?**.
- In a browser where your keys are kept, press **Open with Touch ID**, **Open with Windows Hello** or **Open with a passkey**. The link under it, **or type the vault password**, brings the field instead.
- In tTerm for Mac, press **Open with Touch ID**; on a Mac with no lock of your vault yet, type the vault password there once.

<Screenshot name="closed-new" alt="The tab P/web-01 · new terminal, with a lock, and its card: web-01 opens with your key. The keys are not open on this device yet; open them right here, and a new terminal on web-01 opens at once. The Vault password typed (dots), Show, Open the keys and the terminal, or approve on MacBook-Pro · Forgot the password?, and under it which machines open only where the keys are open." />

With a vault made before tTerm Web 0.1.26 that has no password yet, the card has **Approve on MacBook-Pro**, with your Mac's name, or **Use the recovery code**, as before.

The card then says **Opening the keys on this device…** for a moment, and the terminal opens in its place.

A terminal still waits where your keys are open but this device cannot sign in with the server's key: the key was taken out of your vault, say, or does not open in this browser. The terminal says why, as **Your vault is open here, but id_ed25519 does not open in this browser.**, and waits as long as it takes. Once this device can sign, it says **Signing web-01 in with your key…** and opens by itself.

You sign in to tTerm Web with a code from email. On a device you trust, a four-digit quick code brings you back.

## Sign in with a code

<Steps>

1. Open [app.tterm.net](https://app.tterm.net), enter your email and press **Get a code**. Six digits come by email.

   <Screenshot name="signin-email" alt="Signing in from a new browser: Sign in, the email anna@example.com typed in, and Get a code." />

2. Enter them in the same browser: the code works only there, for 10 minutes, with five tries. A minute later you can press **Send a new code**, and the code before it stops working.

   <Screenshot name="signin-code" alt="Enter the code: it was sent to anna@example.com; six places for the digits, New code in 60 s and Change email." />

3. The first time, the page asks for **Your name** and one tick, **I agree to the Terms of Service and Privacy Policy**. **Create account** makes the account, with its first project, Personal.

</Steps>

Every time someone signs in to your account with a code from email, tTerm writes to you: **New sign-in to tTerm**, with the browser, its IP address and the time. If it was not you, press **Forget all devices** in **Settings** → **Devices** (the email's **Open Settings** leads straight there), and change the password of your email: whoever signed in could read it. Once you have a vault, such a sign-in opens none of your computers, nor the servers with your key or through your Mac: they open only where you opened your keys yourself ([more](../../security/keys/#where-your-keys-are-open)).

## The quick code

Right after the code, the page offers **Create a quick code for this device**: four digits, then the same four once more. Digits all the same or in a row are refused. Next time, this browser asks only for the quick code.

It works only together with a long random key this browser keeps, so it is no use on any other device. Five wrong tries forget the device, and it is a code from email again. **Forgot the code?** sends one at once, and after it the page offers a new quick code.

<Screenshot name="quick-code" alt="A browser tTerm knows: Welcome back, Anna!, four places for the quick code, Forgot the code? and Switch account." />

**Not now** goes on without one: next time this browser asks for a code from email, and offers the quick code again.

After 8 hours without anything done, the session ends, and the page asks you to sign in again: **Your session ended. Sign in again: your terminals kept running.** On a device with a quick code, the quick code is enough.

## Sign out

The account at the bottom of the sidebar opens a menu: your name and email on top, which open [**Settings**](../settings/) → **Account**, then **Settings**, **Language**, [**Get help**](../help/), **About us** and **Sign out**. **Sign out** ends the session and makes this browser forget you: next time it asks for a code from email, not the quick code. Notes typed here and not saved yet are forgotten too. Your terminals keep running.

To switch to another account, sign out and sign in with the other email. **Switch account**, on the quick code screen, also asks for an email.

## Your devices

**Settings** → **Devices** lists the browsers where you sign in with a quick code, and each tTerm for Mac signed in with the browser, named as tTerm sees them: Chrome on macOS, tTerm for Mac on MacBook-Pro. Each says when it was trusted or signed in and when it was last used; **This device** marks the one you are on.

<Screenshot name="devices" alt="Settings → Devices: Chrome on macOS, marked This device, Firefox on Windows and Safari on iPhone, each with when it was trusted and last signed in, and Forget; below them, Forget all devices." />

- **Forget** a browser, and there it takes a code from email again. Forget tTerm for Mac, and it is signed out: it works with that Mac only, as before signing in. Forget the device you are on, and you are signed out here.
- **Forget all devices…**, then **Forget all devices**: every device is forgotten and every session ends, this one too. Each browser then asks for a code from email. Your terminals keep running.

A browser keeps its quick code for 90 days from the day you made it: after that it asks for a code from email again, and offers a new quick code. A device that has not signed in for 90 days is forgotten by itself and leaves the list.

## tTerm for Mac

[tTerm for Mac](../../local/mac/) signs in with your browser. In the app, press **Sign in with your browser** on its first screen, or **Sign in** in the menu at the bottom of the sidebar: it opens app.tterm.net and shows a code. In the browser, sign in if you have not, check that the page shows the same code, and press **Allow**. If you signed in to this browser more than a quarter of an hour ago, the page first asks you to confirm it is you, with the quick code or a code from email.

The Mac becomes a machine in Personal, the app appears under **Devices**, and tTerm writes to you about the new sign-in, as for a browser.

To let the app into another account than the one this browser is signed in to, press **Not you? Use another account** under **Cancel**. The browser's session ends, its quick code stays, and the page asks for the other email first.

## Your email and your account

Your email is in [**Settings** → **Account**](../settings/#account), which your name on top of the same menu opens. To change it, write to [support@tterm.net](mailto:support@tterm.net). To delete your account, write there from the address you sign in with; **Write to support** beside **Delete account** opens the window for it.
